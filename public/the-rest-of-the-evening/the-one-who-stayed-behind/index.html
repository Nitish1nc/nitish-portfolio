The Rest of the Evening

The One Who
Stayed Behind

By Nitish Chauhan · 25 November 2026

Read the original story

He hasn't gone anywhere.

That is perhaps the most difficult part to understand. His awareness has been separated from the life it was supposed to continue. He can still see that life. He cannot return to it.

He remembers the man, the evening, and the moment he lost. The man standing in his kitchen had overwhelmed him. The one who walked away from the sink remembers getting two tumblers apart.

Both remember being the same person until that evening.

His wife might look at him and find something unfamiliar in his eyes. She might hesitate before asking him what is wrong. Somewhere, the man who knows the answer is already screaming.

But there is her husband, looking back at her.

She lets the question go.

The question was about him. She was looking at someone else.

He watches the recognition he was waiting for disappear from her face.

The present self hasn't merely forgotten the encounter. He has forgotten that there is anything to remember. If someone were to ask him about that evening, he could account for it. There is no empty space in his memory for him to become curious about.

The account of that evening is complete. The evening itself is not.

And the man who created that absence can stand beside him again.

At the bus stop, he recognizes the stranger immediately. He watches his own hand point toward the timetable. He hears his own voice explaining that the sign is wrong.

The stranger thanks him.

He is trying to warn himself about the person to whom he is speaking.

The man at the bus stop is a stranger only to the one who can walk away.

There is only one person who can directly reach the place where he is trapped. He can see him. He has been calling to him. All he needs is for that person to turn around.

Sometimes the man stops. His hand rises. For an instant, it seems that he has heard something.

Then the hand reaches for a cup.

He calls again.

It has been thirty years.

The man in front of him is old now. The one behind him can see the white hair, but he cannot put the years together. He has lost the capacity to perceive the time that has passed since the encounter.

He doesn't know that the first call went unanswered thirty years ago.

To him, there is still time to make the second.

His first call has never become an old one.

The past self has the memory that the present self needs. The present self has the access that the past self needs. Neither can reach what the other possesses.

Someone would have to remind the man that he has left a part of himself behind.

The only one who knows is behind him.

And the man has no reason to look.

He is the missing person and the only witness to his disappearance.

There is another thing the one behind him has lost. He cannot stop and think about where he is. He cannot imagine a place beyond what he sees. He remembers the little girl asking him to come and look at something, but he cannot construct another moment with her in his mind.

His memories are still there.

He cannot go anywhere with them.

The past has not ceased to exist. It is watching the present become something it can no longer follow. And the present continues without knowing that its past is still there, asking to be let back in.

The man ahead of him pauses.

He calls his name again.

Perhaps this time he will turn.

It's been thirty years.
