You switch apps a hundred times a day.

Every switch is a small tax: hold, scan, tap, release, hope. Feel the tax for five rounds, then meet the wheel that ends it.

Act 2, why the strip feels bad

The strip is a queue, not a map.

Order follows recency, so the positions reshuffle on every use. Your fingers never learn a place, because the place never stays.

Every switch is a counted tax.

Nine apps in a line means up to eight taps to reach the last one. The strip makes you pay distance in keypresses.

A circle has no ends.

Put the same nine apps on a ring and every app sits one short move from you. Hub topology: stable angles, equal distance, and the wheel turns to meet your hand.

One key, one app.

Pin a badge to every app and switching becomes recognition, not recall. You stop counting presses. You press the key and arrive.

Act 3, ride the wheel

Press Fn, or tap the button, to open the wheel over a mock desktop. Move the pointer and the wheel turns to meet you. Scroll to spin. A badge key or a click lands you. Esc puts it away.
