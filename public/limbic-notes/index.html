01 · THE PROBLEM

The Alt-Tab Tax

Every single extraction from what you're reading costs eight moves:

  1. 01highlight the line
  2. 02⌘C
  3. 03⌘Tab out of the flow
  4. 04hunt the notes window
  5. 05scroll to the bottom
  6. 06⌘V
  7. 07type a timestamp, fix the format
  8. 08⌘Tab back — if you remember where you were

You don't lose focus in one big crash. You lose it in forty small taxes.

THE MATERIAL sample essay · highlight anything

The Anatomy of a Broken Flow

SAMPLE MATERIAL · 4 MIN · HIGHLIGHT ANYTHING

Every reader knows the moment: a sentence lands, and you want to keep it. So you highlight it, copy it, switch apps, hunt for the window, scroll to the bottom, paste, type a timestamp, and switch back. Eight moves. One broken flow state.

Repeat that forty times in a research session and you have not taken notes — you have paid a tax. You don't lose focus in one big crash. You lose it in forty small taxes. The reading never gets the chance to become thinking.

Attention is the asset. Everything else is logistics.

Now imagine the opposite. The sentence lands. You highlight it. You press one arrow key. The text flies to a notebook at the edge of your eye, lands with a soft glow, and you never leave the next sentence. Capture and sorting collapsed into one physical reflex.

The trick is spatial. Top-left for what you love, top-right for what's easy, bottom-right for what's hard, bottom-left for later. Sorting stops being a decision and becomes a direction.

  • A quiet glow confirms delivery — you feel it, you don't read it.
  • Sessions keep projects apart, so nothing bleeds.
  • One notebook can mirror another live, while you edit the twin freely.
  • Notebooks are slots: reminders, clocks and stats can live where notes live.

This is the whole idea: your reading stays in the middle, your notebooks wait at the sides, and your keyboard does the filing. The draft builds itself in your peripheral vision. Now stop reading about it. Highlight something and throw it.
