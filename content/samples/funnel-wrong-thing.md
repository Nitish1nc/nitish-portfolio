Ask a marketing team how the quarter went, and you'll hear two numbers: leads created, and leads converted. Ask a sales team the same question, and you'll hear stories about specific deals. The gap between those two answers is where most B2B funnels quietly fail.

The funnel isn't leaking. It's measuring the wrong thing.

## MQL and SQL are labels, not signals

An MQL is a promise your marketing automation made. An SQL is a promise your sales team made to itself. Neither is a measurement of what the buyer is actually doing. They are internal agreements about when to move a row between columns.

Buyers don't experience your columns. They experience a problem, a budget conversation, a risk calculation, and a moment of readiness. None of those map cleanly to a stage change, which is why the stage change always feels arbitrary to the person responsible for the number.

## Intent is a behavior, not a score

The useful question is not "is this lead qualified?" It is "what is this account doing right now, and what does that behavior suggest they are trying to resolve?"

A few behaviors that out-predict any score:

- Returning to pricing twice in a week, then reading a comparison page. That account is in a budget conversation, not a discovery conversation. Send numbers, not a demo offer.
- A team that downloads an implementation guide but never books a call. They are evaluating effort, not interest. The right content is a migration checklist, not a follow-up sequence.
- Multiple stakeholders from the same domain reading the same technical post. That is consensus forming. The right move is a single, well-argued memo, not three separate sales emails.

Notice the pattern: each behavior suggests the conversation the buyer is already having. Your content's job is to join that conversation, not interrupt it.

## What changes when you measure behavior

Three practical shifts.

1. Report on account conversations, not lead counts. A weekly review of "which accounts are in which conversation, and what did we send to help" produces better decisions than any dashboard of stage movement.

2. Write content for conversations, not keywords. A comparison page, a migration checklist, and a risk memo are not SEO assets first. They are answers to the three conversations every B2B buyer has: what does it cost, what does it take, and what could go wrong.

3. Let sales annotate the funnel. The stories sales tells about deals are the ground truth your stages are approximating. Capture them weekly, and your model of the buyer gets sharper every month.

None of this requires new tooling. It requires deciding that the funnel is a model of the buyer, not a report on the org chart.

Measure the conversation, and the funnel finally tells you something true.
