Most executive teams think they have a visibility problem. They post more, and still nothing moves.

The real problem is point of view.

Scroll your own feed for ten minutes and count how many posts you could have written yourself. Generic optimism. Recycled frameworks. The same three observations about AI, restated with different punctuation. That content is not building authority. It is building immunity. Your audience has learned to skip you.

Authority is not a volume game. It is a positioning game. The executives whose presence compounds do three things differently.

1. They take sides. A point of view with no edge is a press release. Saying "AI will change everything" communicates nothing. Saying "we will not sell you a dashboard, we will sell you infrastructure" costs you some of the audience and earns you the right one.

2. They publish what they learned, not what they launched. Announcements are noise. Judgment is signal. The post about the deal you walked away from, and why, outperforms the post about the product you shipped. Judgment cannot be faked, and it cannot be automated.

3. They write for one reader at a time. "Everyone" is not an audience. A board member, a CFO evaluating vendors, a senior engineer deciding where to work: pick one, and the language gets concrete on its own.

Visibility follows positioning. Not the other way around.

The companies that understand this treat executive content as an asset class: researched, drafted, stress-tested, scheduled. The ones that don't will keep posting more of the same and calling it thought leadership.

It isn't. It's inventory.
