1/ I reviewed 30 AI product launches from the last two months. Different teams, different markets, different branding.

Almost all of them are the same product.

Here is the structural reason why, and where the real edge moved. A thread.

2/ The shared skeleton: input goes in, AI interprets, output comes out. A chat, a summary, a draft. The surface changes. The structure never does.

That structure is now a commodity. Which means the differentiation everyone is fighting over is decoration.

3/ When a layer commoditizes, the leverage moves upstream. It always does. The edge is not in new products. It is in new primitives: the smaller building blocks the next generation of products will be assembled from.

4/ Three primitives I keep seeing underpriced:

- Capture: getting knowledge out of a human at the moment it exists, with zero friction.
- Compression: making stored knowledge denser over time instead of letting it rot in a folder.
- Provenance: proving where a claim came from, which is the actual currency of trust in an AI-saturated feed.

5/ Notice what is missing from that list: generation. Text generation is the cheapest thing in the stack now. Building a company on "we generate X" is building on the one layer where your margin goes to zero.

6/ The investor version of this: stop asking "what does it do?" and ask "which layer does it own?" Products own interfaces. Platforms own primitives. Interfaces get copied in a weekend. Primitives get acquired.

7/ The founder version: your variance is your moat. If your roadmap converges with everyone else's, you don't have a strategy. You have a trend.

8/ I write about this because I build in this space and I'd rather show my reasoning than my logo.

If you're building AI products, tell me which primitive you own. I'll tell you what I'd stress-test first.
